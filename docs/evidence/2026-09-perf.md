# Performance evidence, September 2026

Append-only ledger of measured readings. Tools: `scripts/perf-bench.mjs` (frame bench per tier and GL backend), `scripts/perf-attrib.mjs` (GPU ms with each cost toggled off, needs the timer-query extension), `scripts/perf-probe.mjs` (invariants), `scripts/bake-compare.mjs` (sky + planet captures). "egl" is headless Chromium on the RTX 5070 Ti through ANGLE gl-egl; "swift" is SwiftShader, a CPU rasteriser used as the weak-device proxy (absolute numbers are meaningless, ratios are what count). Every run below is 1280x720 unless stated, in a live belt-clear fight with fire and boost held.

## 2026-09-06 before the pass (commit c77b9b7)

- egl, every tier: 60 fps on vsync, about 1 ms GPU, 0.5 ms sim. The local machine cannot show a bottleneck.
- swift, GPU attribution at 720p: low 76 ms, med 205 ms per frame. Medium's 4x MSAA on the HalfFloat composer target was the single largest cost; bloom about 25 ms; the belt about 26 ms and 331k triangles drawn with no culling; sky and planet each a five-octave fbm per pixel per frame.
- CPU profile (egl, med): nothing above noise except `Asteroids.tick` allocating a four-element array per rock per tick and the HUD writing four style properties every frame.

## 2026-09-06 after the pass

Bench (`perf-bench.mjs tiers=low,med,high gl=egl,swift seconds=5`), run while the chain test and the attribution were also running, so the swift rows carry CPU contention:

| gl | tier | fps | sim ms | render ms (js) | calls | tris |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| egl | low | 59 | 0.52 | 1.62 | 66 | 45k |
| egl | med | 60 | 0.38 | 0.97 | 85 | 46k |
| egl | high | 60 | 0.51 | 1.28 | 85 | 57k |
| swift | low | 10 | 1.28 | 15.30 | 56 | 51k |
| swift | med | 30 | 0.49 | 3.15 | 64 | 101k |
| swift | high | 25 | 0.49 | 4.55 | 64 | 111k |

Triangles drawn fell from about 331k to 45k to 111k per frame across tiers: the belt cull (511 of 2202 instances in the Abydos chase view, `perf-probe.mjs`) is where that comes from. The swift low row was the first tier on a cold browser and overlaps shader compilation; treat it as noise.

GPU attribution on swift at 720p (`perf-attrib.mjs tiers=low,med gl=swift w=1280 h=720`), same contention caveat:

| tier | baseline | bloom off | rocks hidden | shells+lines hidden | sky hidden | planet hidden |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| low | 41.8 ms | 40.6 | 38.5 | 39.9 | 40.7 | 47.9 |
| med | 192.2 ms | 161.0 | 167.5 | 183.3 | 189.8 | 173.6 |

Low: 76 ms to 42 ms. Sky and planet are now within noise of free (they were the fbm shaders). Medium's baseline barely moved under contention; see the clean rerun below.

Bakes: `docs/shots/2026-09-06-bake-{before,after}-{abydos,tollana}.png`, same view, HUD hidden. The pairs match by eye (sky orientation, star field, planet seam and poles, ring). The only differences are rock tumble phase.

Invariants (`perf-probe.mjs`): cursor moves 10 px per dispatched mousemove with the loop stopped (event-rate integration), relative mode stick untouched by the event, dynamic scale 1 → 0.55 after six slow readings and back to 1 after 24 fast ones, off snaps to 1, belt drawn 511 of 2202.

Clean rerun, swift med alone (nothing else on the machine): baseline **152 ms** (from 205 ms before the pass, about a quarter off), bloom off 135, rocks hidden 160, shells and lines hidden 166, sky hidden 161, planet hidden 162, shadows off 186. At this tier the remaining cost is the 2x MSAA resolve and the full-screen passes; the per-object toggles now sit inside the run-to-run noise (about ±10 ms), which is the point: nothing in the scene is a standout any more. The next lever on a device like this is the dynamic resolution, which a headless 720p bench never triggers because the fps reading it adapts to is the browser's, and SwiftShader never reaches 50.

## 2026-09-08 stutters ("stutters and FPS drops, even on low")

Probe: `node scripts/stutter-probe.mjs tiers=low,med,high seconds=25 mission=blockade`, real GPU (`--use-angle=gl-egl`), uncapped with `STUTTER_EXTRA_ARGS="--disable-frame-rate-limit --disable-gpu-vsync"` so a stall shows as its own length and not as a missed vsync. It records every rAF delta, the long-task observer, and with `profile=1` a CPU profile with the busy bursts attributed.

Before (main `de9061e`, the build that was live): worst frame 105 to 121 ms at every tier, several frames over 50 ms in the first fifteen seconds of a blockade fight, all of them shader links. Three's first use of a program calls `getProgramInfoLog`, which waits for the driver to finish linking. The programs that linked mid-fight were the explosion pools, the tracer and fragment instancing variants, the sun flare sprite, and the gate on the win frame; the spawn tick also built the enemy hull geometry.

After (`render/warmup.ts` + `Enemies.prewarm` + render-rate interpolation), same probe:

| tier | fps (uncapped) | p50 | p95 | p99 | max | frames >25 ms | programs at start | programs after the fight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| low | 2413 | 0.4 | 0.8 | 1.1 | 5.6 ms | 0 | 47 | 47 |
| med | 2064 | 0.5 | 0.9 | 1.3 | 9.0 ms | 0 | 56 | 56 |
| high | 2110 | 0.5 | 1.0 | 1.5 | 18.0 ms | 0 | 56 | 56 |

The program count no longer moves during play, which is the invariant that matters: a link that happens at sortie build is hidden behind the arrive fade, a link during play is a frozen frame.

Two wrong turns worth keeping. Drawing the warm frame to the canvas instead of a render target re-linked the tracer, muzzle-flash and flare programs on first use (one for 32 ms): tone mapping and the output colour space are in the program cache key and the real frames draw into the post stack's target. Showing the hidden event-lighting group during the warm draw re-linked the six MeshToon pools and the gunboat hull: the light count is in the key too. The warm draw now leaves lights and their parent groups as they are.

Not measured: a real laptop. The 2026-09-06 SwiftShader proxy rows above still describe the GPU side; this pass removed CPU-side stalls, which weigh more on a weak driver, not less. Event lighting stays off by default and medium is the default tier.

## 2026-09-08 laptop drops ("drops to 30 FPS sometimes and especially on initial load")

Ethan's second report, after the warm-up pass shipped. His machine is a laptop in Chrome. Every reading
above and every reading in this pass is a proxy: a desktop RTX 5070 Ti with CPU throttling for the
processor side, and SwiftShader for the GPU side. Neither is his laptop, and the changes below were chosen
because the mechanism was wrong rather than because a proxy number was bad.

New probe, `node scripts/load-probe.mjs [tiers=..] [gl=egl|swift] [cpu=N] [seconds=N]`. It differs from
`stutter-probe.mjs` in one way that matters here: it records from the first frame instead of skipping the
first three seconds, so the load hitch Ethan named is inside the measurement rather than outside it.

Three mechanisms, all measured before being changed.

**The resolution controller could not act quickly, could not go far enough, and judged the wrong number.**
It waited three seconds, then stepped 0.15 every 1.5 s to a floor of 0.55, so a machine that needed the
floor spent about nine seconds below rate first. On the starved proxy at low it took ten seconds to reach
35 fps. Worse, it read the loop's mean frame rate, which is blind in both directions: frames alternating
16.7 and 33.3 ms average to 40 fps while the picture visibly hitches, and one 883 ms load stall reads as a
catastrophically slow machine and costs resolution for the rest of the sortie. It now reads the window's
second-worst frame time, takes its first reading at 0.6 s, jumps straight to the scale the measurement
implies, and floors at 0.35.

**The sky and planet bakes ran at the tier's full size inside the first frame**, before anything knew what
the machine could afford: six procedural cube faces plus an equirect surface, at 1024 on medium. The first
bake is now capped at 256 and the real size follows the settled resolution scale.

**Below the floor there was nothing left to give.** A laptop that cannot hold 60 at 0.35 was simply left at
30. It now drops the bloom pass, and takes it back before it takes back any pixels. Bloom was the right
thing to give up and shadows was not: `shadowMap.enabled` is in every program's cache key, so toggling it
mid-fight would relink the scene, which is the exact stall `render/warmup.ts` exists to prevent.

Readings, before and after, same build ordering, `mission=blockade`:

| proxy | tier | steady fps | gpu ms | worst frame | when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| swift, no CPU throttle | low | 27 → 38 | 23.4 → 24.3 | 350 → 333 ms | 1.34 s → 1.28 s |
| swift, no CPU throttle | med | 13 → 24 | 56.9 → 41.3 | 6683 → 6516 ms | 8.09 s → 8.22 s |
| RTX 5070 Ti, 4x CPU | med | 60 → 60 | 1.2 → 0.5 | 883 → 667 ms | 1.31 s → 0.78 s |

The CPU-throttled row is the one that speaks to "especially on initial load": the whole load now completes
before the first second instead of stalling into the second, and the twenty seconds after it hold 60 fps
with four frames over 33 ms. The remaining 667 ms is warm-up and the first bake, and it happens before the
first frame is presented, so it reads as loading rather than as a stutter.

The multi-second SwiftShader medium frame is not a laptop signal. Between 1.4 s and 8.2 s that proxy draws
essentially nothing: it is a software rasteriser compiling every program the warm-up asks for. Any real
GPU, integrated ones included, links those in a fraction of the time, which is what the 667 ms row shows.

Load attribution, `src/core/timing.ts`, at 4x CPU throttle: bundle evaluation 507 ms, then the sortie build
258 ms of which the mission and combat graph is 131 ms, the world 74 ms and the parametric ship 49 ms. That
module measures the main thread only. Wrapping a bake or a draw with it reports 0 ms, because handing
commands to the driver is not the same as the driver executing them; the header says so.

Two traps found while building this, both now covered by the invariants.

The bake size cannot follow the scale continuously. The controller's 0.05 steps straddle the power-of-two
boundaries, so a scale drifting between 0.70 and 0.75 would flip the medium tier between a 512 and a 1024
sky and re-bake six cube faces and an equirect surface on every crossing. It is latched at the first
reading instead and never moves again for that page.

The capture scripts had no resolution pin. The old controller waited three seconds before its first
reading, so a 1.5 s capture was always at full resolution by construction; at 0.6 s it no longer is, and a
slow headless run would have quietly photographed a reduced-resolution frame, or one with bloom off, into
the reference images. `?dynres=0` pins the tier's full resolution with every effect on, and
`capture-shots.mjs`, `hull-shots.mjs` and `bake-compare.mjs` all pass it. The bake captures after the change
(`docs/shots/2026-09-08-bake-{abydos,tollana}.png`) are unchanged by eye against the 2026-09-06 pair: sharp
planet terminator, nebula banding, rock outlines.

A third trap, caught but not in the code: `bake-compare.mjs after` writes over the dated `2026-09-06-bake-after-*`
reference images, which is how a comparison loses the thing it compares against. Today's pair is written under
today's date instead.

Invariants (`perf-probe.mjs`, rewritten for this controller and run with the loop stopped so its own frames
do not contaminate a window): steady 30 fps drops the scale to 0.70, a good stretch climbs it back, one
900 ms stall inside a clean window costs nothing, one frame in ten at 33 ms drops it to 0.70, the floor is
0.35 and then bloom goes off, bloom returns before the scale does, the first bake is 256, and the size
latched at the first reading survives the scale climbing back. Two were watched failing first: with the
relief step stubbed out the check reports `floorRelief 0`, and with the latch removed it reports
`bakeAfterClimb 1024` against a settled 512.

Still not measured: Ethan's laptop. The overlay reading to ask him for when it next drops is `fps`, `res`,
`gpu` and whether `bloom off (perf)` is showing, which together say whether he is fill-bound, CPU-bound, or
neither.
